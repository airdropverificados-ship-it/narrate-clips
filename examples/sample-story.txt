The lighthouse keeper lit the lamp every night without fail, even after the village stopped paying him and the ships stopped coming. The ships far out at sea depended on that single point of light to find their way home safely through the fog. One October night the lamp went dark for the first time in forty years, and by morning three boats had vanished from the harbour without a trace. The keeper swore he had lit the lamp as always, but the oil was untouched and the wick was cold. Nobody in the village would walk up the hill after that, and the lighthouse has stood dark ever since.
